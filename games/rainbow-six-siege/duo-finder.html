Why a purpose-built duo finder

  • Operator pool depth matters — a Diamond Attacker who only mains Ash is a liability when the opponent has Bandit + Mute locking down the reinforced walls. Siege stacks need overlapping operator pools, not single-op identity.
  • Scrim culture: serious Siege teams train in Custom Games more than ranked. A team finder needs a 'scrim-available' flag that Valorant or LoL boards never grew.
  • The map ban phase requires team-level coordination — an attacker-favored stack and a defender-favored stack veto opposite maps and self-cancel.
  • Ranked 3.0's division ladder means a 'Diamond IV' and a 'Diamond II' player sit further apart than the shared label suggests, and only two ranks of that distance are queue-legal at their tier.

Whatever kind of player you are

You build teams to climb

Every Siege season soft-resets RP, and the June 2026 Ranked 3.0 overhaul made the ladder even less forgiving to stale rosters: Copper through Emerald squad within three full ranks, Diamond and Champion within two, and squad balancing weights your team toward its highest-RP player. The five you ended last season with may now be queue-illegal together.

Jynx reads the current patch of your record, not the peak: every profile carries a verified RP division, and matching only proposes players whose division keeps your squad inside Ubisoft's window. Operator-pool depth is the second gate — hard breacher, entry, anchor, roamer, and IGL fill as five distinct slots, not four copies of your main.

Spend each season's five placement matches queueing together — restrictions do not apply there, and squads that place together seed together. From the first ranked round onward, recruit inside your division band, then move the core's practice into Custom Game scrims two nights a week before Diamond narrows the window to two ranks.

You play for the people

Siege's best moments are shouted — a Caveira interrogate ping, a wallbang nobody believes, an overtime clutch at 2 AM. But Quick Match lobbies empty after one map, Standard teams disband at the score screen, and the Discord recruitment channel that was fun on Tuesday has scrolled past you by Thursday night.

Jynx keeps the people worth keeping: voice-intent matching puts mic-on banter squads together and text-first players with each other, while a recurring-stack setting turns one good Standard night into a standing Thursday five — no tryout culture, no rank gate, just Siege players whose humor and weeknights align.

Let the group marinate in Standard while callouts and map preferences surface; ranked punishes a five still learning each other's rotations far harder than casual does. When three of you start finishing callouts mid-sentence, take that exact five into ranked — Siege forgives missed shots far less often than it forgives silence.

You grind alone — until you don't have to

Solo queue in Siege means four strangers who never drone their entry, insta-lock operators, and leave you anchoring site alone on every defense round. Carrying works through Gold, but retake math demands a trade partner, and range time cannot replicate someone playing off your Bandit trick or holding your crossfire.

Jynx respects the lone-wolf streak: voice stays optional, there is no clan paperwork, and matching only runs when you ask it to. It pairs you with other queue-alone players who want exactly one filtered partner for a ranked push, and the roster stays at that single verified duo until a second genuinely earns a slot.

Audition a partner in Standard, never ranked: one casual map exposes rotations, timeout habits, and tilt faster than a week of chat. Keep one anchor-side and one entry-side duo saved for nights your usual pick is offline, and re-queue ranked only with the teammate whose crossfires you already trust.

You're coming back after a break

Walk back in after a year away and Siege has moved underneath you: Ranked 3.0 rewired progression around RP, Champion grew five divisions, half your operator pool was reworked, and the ban phase now vetoes maps you never learned to veto. The old Crew disbanded two patches ago, and recruitment channels read a decayed rank as your ceiling.

Jynx profiles carry verified rank history, so a lapsed Diamond reads as lapsed rather than fictional, and the returning filter pairs you with stacks rebuilding too — players who expect rust and whose operator pools cover your gaps while your aim comes back. No fresh-Copper treatment for someone two seasons off peak.

Relearn two site setups per map plus the current ban meta in Standard during week one, then re-enter through the next season's five placement matches, when squad restrictions lift — the cleanest re-entry window the ladder offers. A steady partner halves the climb back toward where you left.

How to find an R6 Siege team that scrims, not just ranks

  1. Match operator pool overlap, not single operator — A Thatcher main and a Maverick main both cover hard-breach utility — they're compatible. A Thatcher main and another Thatcher main fight for the same role. Filter for 3+ overlapping ops, not single-op identity.
  2. Filter for scrim availability — If you're serious about climbing past Diamond, you need scrims. A duo who's 'ranked only' is fine for Platinum-Emerald but won't get to Champion. Flag 'open to scrim 1-2 nights/week' on your profile.
  3. Same-region servers, always — Siege ping under 60ms is critical for pixel peeks and lean angles. A NA-EU duo will share 90+ ping every match — playable but always a degradation.
  4. Find your IGL early — Siege above Platinum is strat-heavy. A 5-stack without a designated IGL plays default every round and loses to teams who plan executes. Listing 'IGL' or 'looking for IGL' as a role tag accelerates team formation.

Duo finder FAQ

How do you find a solid R6 Siege team?
Start from the squad limits: Copper through Emerald can queue within three full ranks, Diamond and Champion only two. Match inside that window first, then filter for operator-pool overlap — three attackers and three defenders each — before layering voice intent and scrim nights. Stacks assembled in that order survive placements.
Do I need a full 5-stack for ranked, or is a duo enough?
A duo plus three randoms reaches Platinum or Emerald comfortably. Pushing Diamond and Champion reliably needs a stable three — Siege strats lean on mid-round coordination solo queues never provide. Build the duo first, add a third through the same filtered search, and keep the fifth slot for a dedicated IGL.
How do I find an R6 IGL?
Most boards bury strat-callers under loose role tags. A purpose-built finder lets you tag IGL as your primary role and search stacks listing 'looking for IGL' — a filter only a small slice of ranked players qualify for, so it narrows the pool in your favor. Trial callers in Standard before handing them ranked rounds.
Can I filter for attack and defense roles separately?
Yes — and in Siege you should. You spend half each map on each side, so a teammate who anchors defense but fills attack with Sledge covers two slots. Jynx filters operator pools per side, pairing hard-breach support with entry fraggers and roamers with anchors instead of stacking five players who main the same three operators.

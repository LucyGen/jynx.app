About Arthur

I'm Arthur, co-founder of Jynx. I grew up in France losing entire weekends to LAN parties, then watching Discord LFG threads slowly turn into a soup of bots, recruiters, and toxic randoms. Jynx is what I wished existed back then: a place where the matchmaking actually understands you — your rank, your schedule, the way you communicate when things go sideways at round 14.

My background is in product. Before Jynx I spent years building consumer products and obsessing over the moment a stranger first opens an app and decides whether to stay. That's the moment we live or die on, and it's why every screen in Jynx is reviewed against one question: would a tilted ranked player at 1am still tap "next"? If the answer is no, we cut it.

On the gaming side — I'm a long-time League of Legends and Valorant player. Peak rank in each, current Riot ID, and approximate hours played are listed in the credentials grid below (some still pending public confirmation). I've also rotated through the usual suspects: CS2, Apex, the Halo MCC coop run, and (occasionally, against my better judgment) Pokémon Unite. I write here mostly about matchmaking design, the failure modes of solo queue, and what I've personally learned about finding teammates who don't ghost you after a single bad map.

What I care about, and why I write: I think the LFG layer of gaming has been broken for fifteen years. Discord servers don't scale, in-game LFG is anonymous and adversarial, and Reddit's /r/LFG is either spam or a graveyard. The thesis behind Jynx is that better data + opt-in personality + skill verification beats any amount of "be nice in chat" moderation. Most of what I publish here is field notes from building that — what's working, what's embarrassingly broken, and what we're shipping next.

When I'm not in the app, I'm usually queueing duo, reviewing match-flow drafts, or losing to my own algorithm.

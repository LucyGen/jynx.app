LFG Tools in 2026: A Comparative Snapshot of Where Gamers Look for Teammates

A synthesis of public data on the major Looking-For-Group platforms gamers use today, drawn from app-store rankings, Steam concurrent-user data, and platform-published statistics.

Last updated May 8, 2026 Published May 8, 2026 Author: Jynx Research

Key findings

  • Discord remains the dominant default — its 200M+ monthly active users include the largest aggregated LFG audience even though Discord itself does not market 'LFG' as a product surface.
  • Reddit's r/Fortnite, r/VALORANT, r/leagueoflegends and r/DestinyTheGame each report 1M+ subscribers; per-subreddit LFG threads and dedicated LFG subreddits surface daily activity in the tens of thousands.
  • Web-first LFG sites (op.gg, tracker.gg) draw most of their traffic from stat-lookup tools, with LFG features layered on top — meaning their LFG audience is a strict subset of their measured visitor base.
  • Dedicated LFG apps (Jynx, GamerLink, RiftQ) operate at App Store ranking depth — visible in the gaming social subcategory but with smaller absolute installs than the multipurpose platforms above.
  • No single platform owns the LFG vertical; the audience is fragmented across Discord servers, game-specific subreddits, web tools, and apps — which is precisely why dedicated LFG apps continue to launch.

Methodology

We compiled this snapshot from publicly available sources only (no paid panels, no proprietary surveys). For each platform we recorded the most recent published metric the platform itself or a public ranking site disclosed. Data points are categorised as: (a) self-reported user/MAU figures from the company, (b) third-party measurement (App Store rankings, SimilarWeb, Steam Charts), or (c) public Reddit subscriber counts. The chart compares relative scale on a log-friendly basis because the numbers span four orders of magnitude. We do not attempt to convert these into 'LFG-active users' — most of these tools are general-purpose and only a fraction of their audience is actively looking for teammates at any given moment. The intent of the page is to surface where the LFG audience plausibly lives so writers, researchers, and product teams can prioritise their attention.

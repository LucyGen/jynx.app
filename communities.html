Frequently asked questions

What are Jynx communities?

+

Communities are the clubs inside the Jynx app: player-run groups built around one game or a shared set of games. Each has a member count, an activity level, and a join mode, and joining one puts you in a room with people who already play what you play. This directory lists every public community from the October 2026 census.

How many communities are there?

+

The October 2026 census counted 284 named public communities across 177 different games — plus a global Jynx community of 20,423 members that every player can join. The directory below groups them into 56 sections so you can browse by game.

How do I join a community?

+

In the Jynx app. Most communities are open — tap Join and you're straight in — while some require approval from an organizer, which keeps competitive groups coherent. Browse this directory to find your game, open the community in the app, and join from its page.

Which games have the biggest communities?

+

By combined membership in the October 2026 census, the biggest constellations are League of Legends, Minecraft, Valorant, Free Fire, Roblox, and Fortnite — each with thousands of members across a dozen or more communities. Wild Rift and Marvel Rivals round out the top eight, and the directory's 56 sections cover everything else.

Can I create my own community?

+

Yes — anyone can start one in the Jynx app, free. Name it, pick its games, and choose whether joining is open or approval-required. Organizers can grow their club with boosts, coaching, and streaming: the census already counted more than a hundred coach-carrying communities and a handful of dedicated streamer clubs, so the pattern works.

How is community activity measured?

+

Every listing shows an activity label — Very active, Active, Growing, or Brand new — baked from the October 2026 census: each community's activity level and its most recent recorded activity. Communities with fewer than ten members show a New badge instead of a count. The census refreshes quarterly, so labels and counts always name their edition rather than pretending to be live.
